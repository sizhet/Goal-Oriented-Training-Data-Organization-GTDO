# Constitution of Goal-Oriented Training Data Organization

## GTDO Constitutional Principles

**Repository:** Goal-Oriented Training Data Organization
**Abbreviation:** GTDO
**Primary Scope:** Training Data Organization, Computational Responsibility, Dispatch, Brain-Unit Formation, and General AI Computation Organization
**Status:** Constitutional Anchor

---

## 1. Purpose of This Constitution

This Constitution defines the stable conceptual boundaries, architectural principles, terminology, and research discipline of Goal-Oriented Training Data Organization.

GTDO is not merely a data-cleaning method, a clustering technique, a segmentation algorithm, or an LLM-routing mechanism.

GTDO studies how data, context, goals, computational responsibilities, computational units, dispatch structures, and optimization scopes can be organized into an explicit and controllable AI computation architecture.

All major GTDO texts, figures, algorithms, engineering designs, and repository extensions should remain consistent with this Constitution.

When a new topic does not fit the existing structure, the preferred process is:

1. identify the conceptual gap;
2. determine whether the new topic extends GTDO;
3. update the constitutional outline;
4. place the new work within the revised structure;
5. preserve consistency across terminology, figures, and navigation files.

The repository structure is not merely administrative.

It is part of the theory.

---

## 2. Foundational Position

GTDO begins from the observation that training data is commonly treated as a largely unified corpus to be cleaned, tokenized, sampled, shuffled, and supplied to a model.

This workflow leaves a major control-engineering question insufficiently addressed:

> How should training data be organized according to the computational responsibilities that future AI structures are expected to perform?

GTDO introduces an explicit organization layer between raw data and computational capability formation.

The general transformation is:

```text
Raw Data and Context
        ↓
Goal Function
        ↓
Structural Recognition
        ↓
Grouping and Boundary Analysis
        ↓
Computational Responsibility Assignment
        ↓
Dispatch Structure
        ↓
Computational Unit Formation
        ↓
Localized Training, Evaluation, and Evolution
```

GTDO therefore treats training data organization as a mechanism for organizing future computation.

---

## 3. Goal Orientation Is Primary

Every GTDO organization process must be governed by an explicit or recoverable goal.

The goal may be represented by:

* an RHS grouping objective;
* a target output family;
* a task responsibility;
* a structural distinction;
* a domain boundary;
* a control condition;
* a runtime requirement;
* an optimization objective;
* a safety requirement;
* a human-defined organizational purpose.

The grouping algorithm is not primary.

The goal is primary.

A mathematically elegant grouping is not sufficient if it does not improve the intended computational organization.

A GTDO process must therefore answer:

```text
What distinction matters?

Why does it matter?

Which computational responsibility does it create?

Which computational structure should receive that responsibility?

How will the resulting organization be validated?
```

Goal functions may be binary, multi-group, hierarchical, continuous, structural, contextual, or dynamically updated.

The simplest case may separate two RHS groups and optionally produce a boundary or discard group.

GTDO must not be restricted to binary grouping.

---

## 4. Organization Stands Above Segmentation

Segmentation and organization are related but distinct.

Segmentation answers:

> Where should data be divided?

Organization answers:

> Which computational structure should take responsibility for this data, context, task, or goal?

Segmentation produces boundaries.

Dispatch assigns responsibility.

A GTDO grouping may contain non-contiguous samples distributed across a corpus, sequence, space, or time range.

Therefore, valid computation organization does not require contiguous segmentation.

The canonical distinction is:

```text
Segmentation
    ↓
Creates data regions

Dispatch
    ↓
Assigns computational responsibility

Organization
    ↓
Creates a controllable computation architecture
```

Segmentation may support GTDO.

Segmentation does not define GTDO.

GTDO treats training data organization as a control layer for future computation.

The direct output of GTDO is not merely a collection of data partitions.

Its outputs may include:

* candidate computational responsibilities;
* specialized training groups;
* boundary sets;
* discard sets;
* fallback groups;
* computational-unit specifications;
* dispatch rules;
* dispatch trees;
* dispatch graphs;
* call paths;
* call-path segments;
* local optimization scopes;

The organization of training data influences:

* which capabilities are formed;
* where capabilities are located;
* how capabilities are invoked;
* how they cooperate;
* how they are improved;
* how failures are isolated;
* how updates are verified.

GTDO is therefore a bridge between data organization and AI architecture.

## 7. Computational Responsibility Is a First-Class Concept

A GTDO group should represent more than statistical proximity.

It should correspond, directly or indirectly, to a computational responsibility.

Computational responsibility may include:

* producing a target output;
* recognizing a structural condition;
* solving a task family;
* handling a context family;
* performing a transformation;
* validating another unit;
* controlling a process;
* handling uncertain or boundary cases;
* acting as a fallback;
* coordinating other units.

A data group becomes architecturally meaningful when it supports the formation, selection, training, or validation of a computational unit with a defined responsibility.

The preferred progression is:

```text
Data Group
    ↓
Responsibility Candidate
    ↓
Computational Unit
    ↓
Dispatchable Capability
```

GTDO must avoid creating arbitrary groups without a clear computational interpretation.

## 8. Two Core Grouping Modes

GTDO recognizes two foundational grouping modes.

### 8.1 Point-to-Group Assignment

In Point-to-Group Assignment, samples assigned to the same group are not required to occupy a continuous region.

Examples include:

* structurally similar cases appearing at distant corpus locations;
* common RHS outcomes produced by different contexts;
* cross-document domain grouping;
* functionally equivalent samples;
* distributed examples assigned to one computational responsibility.

The canonical structural algorithm for this mode is:

> Two-Way Common Concept Core

Two-Way CCC may be used to recognize two dominant structural directions and assign samples to corresponding groups.

Point-to-Group Assignment may produce:

* Group A;
* Group B;
* Boundary Set;
* Discard Set;
* deferred or unresolved cases.

### 8.2 Point-to-Block Grouping

In Point-to-Block Grouping, the grouped region must preserve continuity or ordered adjacency.

Examples include:

* contiguous text regions;
* context windows;
* temporal intervals;
* local event sequences;
* spatial regions;
* code blocks;
* ordered training segments.

The canonical structural algorithm for this mode is:

> Variable-Size Blocks Indexing and Searching

Point-to-Block Grouping preserves the organization of variable-length regions while allowing efficient indexing, recognition, and dispatch.

These two modes are complementary.

Neither should be treated as universally superior.

The appropriate mode is determined by the structure of the goal.

---

## 9. Boundary Sets Are First-Class Computational Objects

A GTDO grouping process must not assume that all samples can be assigned confidently to dominant groups.

The unresolved remainder should not automatically be treated as waste.

The preferred term is:

> Boundary Set

A Boundary Set may contain:

* noise;
* malformed data;
* weakly relevant samples;
* mixed-context samples;
* cross-domain samples;
* transitional structures;
* insufficiently represented structures;
* future independent domains;
* cases requiring multi-unit cooperation.

Boundary data may represent unresolved computation organization rather than failed data processing.

GTDO must distinguish at least the following outcomes:

```text
Confident Group Assignment
Boundary Assignment
Discard Assignment
Fallback Assignment
Deferred Assignment
Multi-Path Assignment
```

Boundary handling is part of the primary GTDO architecture.

It is not an implementation afterthought.

---

## 10. Weak Separation Must Not Be Forced

Two-Way CCC or any other grouping algorithm may encounter data whose separation is not significant.

GTDO must not manufacture artificial binary structure merely because a binary algorithm is available.

When the separation signal is weak, acceptable actions include:

* retaining the original group;
* assigning the data to a Boundary Set;
* applying recursive Two-Way CCC under a revised goal or context;
* forming a Boundary Brain Unit;
* using a general fallback computational unit;
* using the pre-grouping model as a fallback;
* applying multi-path runtime dispatch;
* deferring organization until more evidence is available;
* discarding only data identified as low-value or invalid.

The decision must be controlled by significance, confidence, cost, and computational responsibility.

Forced grouping can damage both training quality and architectural clarity.

---

## 11. Recursive Organization Is Permitted but Must Be Controlled

Boundary Sets or broad groups may be reorganized recursively.

The general form is:

```text
Initial Group
      ↓
Goal-Oriented Separation
      ↓
Primary Groups + Boundary Set
                         ↓
               Revised Goal or Context
                         ↓
              Secondary Organization
```

Recursive grouping may improve structural resolution.

However, recursion must have explicit stopping conditions.

Possible stopping criteria include:

* insufficient separation significance;
* minimum group size;
* insufficient training value;
* excessive computational cost;
* unstable group membership;
* low validation improvement;
* violation of continuity constraints;
* unacceptable loss of generality;
* saturation of structural gain;
* reaching a predefined organization depth.

Recursive organization is not automatically beneficial.

The objective is meaningful computation organization, not maximal fragmentation.

---

## 12. Fallback Is a Required Architectural Principle

A reliable GTDO system must retain a fallback path.

Fallback may be provided by:

* a general model trained on pre-grouping data;
* a general LLM;
* a Boundary Brain Unit;
* a multi-domain computational unit;
* a retrieval or reasoning system;
* a human review unit;
* a runtime multi-path mechanism;
* a previous stable system version.

Fallback is required because:

* some data will remain unresolved;
* contexts may differ from training conditions;
* dispatch confidence may be low;
* specialized units may fail;
* organization boundaries may drift;
* new task families may appear.

A GTDO system without fallback is structurally brittle.

Specialization must not eliminate general coverage.

## 13. Context Is Part of the Organization Problem

GTDO may use context from both offline and online sources.

Offline context may include:

* neighboring training data;
* document structure;
* metadata;
* domain labels;
* source relationships;
* historical usage;
* generated structural summaries;
* known calling relationships;
* known computational outcomes.

Online context may include:

* user intent;
* runtime state;
* active task;
* previous calls;
* available computational units;
* resource constraints;
* confidence signals;
* environmental conditions;
* current system goals.

Context may change the assignment of the same nominal input.

Therefore, dispatch should not be reduced to a fixed mapping from isolated samples to static groups.

The more general relation is:

```text
Input + Context + Goal + Runtime State
                    ↓
              Dispatch Decision
```

Context generation itself may be a GTDO-supported computational responsibility.

---

## 14. Brain Units Are Important but Not Universal

A training group may become a candidate Brain Unit.

A Brain Unit is a computational structure with a bounded or recognizable responsibility that can be trained, invoked, evaluated, versioned, and improved with some degree of independence.

Possible Brain Units include:

* domain units;
* task units;
* context units;
* validation units;
* planning units;
* boundary units;
* fallback units;
* control units;
* coordination units.

However, not every group must become an independent model.

A group may instead produce:

* an adapter;
* a routing rule;
* a prompt structure;
* a retrieval collection;
* a symbolic operator;
* a function;
* a policy;
* a shared parameter region;
* a validation rule;
* a call-path constraint.

GTDO organizes computational responsibility.

The physical implementation of that responsibility remains an engineering decision.

---

## 15. Dispatch Trees Are Calling Structures

A hierarchical GTDO organization may form a Dispatch Tree.

Each branch identifies a sequence of increasingly specific computational responsibilities.

A root-to-node or root-to-leaf sequence can be interpreted as a:

> Call Path

A contiguous portion of that sequence can be interpreted as a:

> Call Path Segment

Example:

```text
General Dispatcher
        ↓
Engineering Unit
        ↓
Software Unit
        ↓
Compiler Unit
        ↓
Optimization Unit
```

This sequence is simultaneously:

* a dispatch route;
* a responsibility decomposition;
* a training specialization path;
* a runtime call path;
* a local optimization scope.

When multiple branches share computational units, the structure may evolve from a tree into a Directed Acyclic Graph or a more general dispatch graph.

GTDO must therefore support both:

* Dispatch Trees;
* Hybrid Computation Dispatch Graphs.

---

## 16. Call Paths Are Optimization Scopes

In a Brain-Unit-based or heterogeneous Hybrid AI system, the optimization target need not be the entire model.

It may instead be:

* a whole Call Path;
* a Call Path prefix;
* a Call Path suffix;
* a Call Path Segment;
* a single computational unit;
* a shared subgraph;
* a dispatch decision;
* a boundary-resolution policy.

This enables:

* focused reinforcement learning;
* localized fine-tuning;
* selective freezing;
* branch-specific evaluation;
* controlled reward propagation;
* local versioning;
* local rollback;
* reduced interference;
* improved traceability;
* lower retraining cost.

A central GTDO principle is:

> In an organized Hybrid AI system, optimization should be applied at the smallest structurally valid scope that can achieve the intended improvement.

Whole-model optimization remains available but should not be treated as the only optimization mode.

## 17. Organization Must Preserve General Capability

Specialization can improve precision and efficiency, but excessive decomposition can damage general capability.

GTDO must preserve a balance among:

* specialization;
* shared knowledge;
* cross-domain reasoning;
* general fallback;
* transferability;
* maintainability;
* computational economy.

A GTDO architecture should not isolate units so strongly that they can no longer cooperate.

The system should support:

* shared computational units;
* shared structural assets;
* cross-path calls;
* multi-unit validation;
* multi-path reasoning;
* general fallback;
* capability recombination.

The objective is organized intelligence, not fragmented intelligence.

## 18. Organization Must Be Verifiable

Every GTDO transformation should expose a validation path.

Validation may occur at:

* sample level;
* group level;
* block level;
* Brain Unit level;
* dispatch level;
* Call Path level;
* graph level;
* whole-system level.

Relevant validation measures may include:

* grouping significance;
* structural coherence;
* assignment stability;
* boundary-set size;
* discard quality;
* training efficiency;
* output quality;
* path accuracy;
* dispatch confidence;
* coverage;
* latency;
* computational cost;
* failure isolation;
* rollback success;
* general capability preservation.

GTDO should favor explicit and inspectable organization over opaque partitioning.

A group that cannot be explained, evaluated, or connected to a computational responsibility should be treated cautiously.

## 19. Local Evolution Must Be Supported

A GTDO architecture should allow one part of the system to improve without requiring uncontrolled modification of the entire system.

Local evolution may include:

* retraining one Brain Unit;
* replacing one computational unit;
* changing one dispatch rule;
* refining one Boundary Set;
* revising one Call Path Segment;
* adding one new domain branch;
* merging redundant branches;
* splitting an overloaded responsibility;
* updating context-generation logic;
* rolling back one local version.

Local evolution must preserve compatibility with surrounding structures.

This requires explicit:

* interfaces;
* responsibilities;
* validation criteria;
* dependency relationships;
* version identifiers;
* rollback points.

GTDO therefore supports AI systems that evolve through controlled structural change rather than repeated whole-system replacement.

Core operators may include:

New operators should be added only when existing operators cannot express the required organization cleanly.

The framework should avoid unnecessary mechanism proliferation.

A small operator set with broad structural coverage is preferred.

## 21. GTDO and Calling Graphs

GTDO and Calling Graphs address complementary organization problems.

GTDO organizes:

* which computational responsibilities should exist;
* which data and contexts support those responsibilities;
* which computational units should own them;
* how those units should be grouped and optimized.

Calling Graphs organize:

* when computational units are invoked;
* in what order they are invoked;
* how inputs and outputs flow;
* how computation is executed.

The relationship can be summarized as:

> GTDO organizes computational responsibility.
> Calling Graphs organize computational execution.

A mature AI system may use GTDO to form its computational organization and Calling Graphs to operate that organization.

## 24. Research Discipline

GTDO research should clearly distinguish among:

* established mechanisms;
* inherited algorithms;
* proposed extensions;
* engineering hypotheses;
* experimental findings;
* long-term forecasts.

Claims should be labeled according to their level of support.

Existing algorithms such as Two-Way CCC and Variable-Size Blocks Indexing and Searching should be described accurately and connected to GTDO without obscuring their original functions.

New proposals such as Call-Path Reinforcement Learning should be presented as explicit extensions when experimental validation is not yet available.

Conceptual ambition should be matched by structural clarity.

## 26. Canonical Terms

The following terms should be used consistently.

### Goal Function

A function, rule, objective, or structural requirement that determines the intended organization.

### Training Data Organization

The process of organizing training data according to goals and future computational responsibilities.

### Computational Responsibility

The task, capability, recognition function, transformation, validation role, or control duty assigned to a computational unit.

### Dispatch

The process of assigning computational responsibility or computational work to an appropriate unit.

### Segmentation

The process of dividing data into regions or blocks.

### Point-to-Group Assignment

Assignment of potentially non-contiguous samples to a common group.

### Point-to-Block Grouping

Grouping that preserves region continuity or ordered adjacency.

### Boundary Set

A set of unresolved, mixed, transitional, weakly separated, or multi-responsibility cases.

### Computational Unit

Any model, function, algorithm, tool, agent, database, expert, or hybrid structure capable of taking computational responsibility.

### Brain Unit

A computational unit with a bounded or recognizable AI responsibility.

### Dispatch Tree

A hierarchical organization of dispatch decisions and computational responsibilities.

### Dispatch Graph

A generalized dispatch structure with shared units or non-tree connections.

### Call Path

A sequence of computational units or dispatch decisions traversed for a task.

### Call Path Segment

A contiguous subsection of a Call Path.

### Fallback

A general or alternate computational route used when specialized dispatch is uncertain or unsuccessful.

### Local Optimization

Optimization restricted to a structurally defined unit, path, segment, or subgraph.

## 28. Closing Principle

GTDO begins with training data, but it does not end with training data.

Its deeper subject is the organization of computation.

Its central transition is:

```text
Data Partition
      ↓
Structural Group
      ↓
Computational Responsibility
      ↓
Dispatchable Unit
      ↓
Call Path
      ↓
Controllable AI Organization
```

The long-term objective of GTDO is not to create more partitions.

It is to make AI computation more explicit, specialized, cooperative, verifiable, locally optimizable, and structurally evolvable.

> The purpose of GTDO is to transform training data organization into a controllable architecture of computational responsibility.
