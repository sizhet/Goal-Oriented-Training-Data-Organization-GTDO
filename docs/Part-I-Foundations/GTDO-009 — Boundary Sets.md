# GTDO-009 — Boundary Sets

## Boundary Sets as First-Class Organizational Objects in AI Computation Organization

---

## Abstract

Classical machine learning usually assumes that every training sample should eventually belong to one category.

Goal-Oriented Training Data Organization (GTDO) adopts a different view.

When computational organization is driven by Goal Functions rather than by simple statistical similarity, some samples naturally remain outside stable organizational groups.

These samples are not necessarily noise.

They may represent:

* insufficient evidence,
* incomplete context,
* competing Computational Responsibilities,
* transitional structures,
* cross-domain computation,
* emerging capabilities,
* architectural limitations,
* or future computational opportunities.

GTDO therefore introduces **Boundary Sets** as a first-class organizational object.

Boundary Sets are not failures of organization.

They are explicit representations of unresolved computation organization.

Instead of forcing uncertain assignments, GTDO preserves organizational uncertainty, allowing later refinement through additional context, revised Goal Functions, recursive organization, Human–AI collaboration, or architectural evolution.

Boundary Sets become one of the most important control-engineering mechanisms within GTDO.

---

# 1. Why Boundary Sets Exist

Real-world computation is rarely cleanly separable.

Even when an objective appears binary,

```text
Accept
Reject
```

many cases naturally fall between them.

Examples include:

* partially matching structures,
* multiple simultaneous goals,
* insufficient context,
* conflicting evidence,
* unknown runtime conditions,
* emerging concepts.

Therefore, a realistic organizational result is

```text
Training Data
        │
        ▼
Goal-Oriented Organization
        │
 ┌──────┼──────────┐
 │      │          │
 ▼      ▼          ▼
Group A Group B Boundary Set
```

The Boundary Set is an expected organizational output.

It should never be viewed merely as "remaining data."

---

# 2. Canonical Definition

Within GTDO,

> **A Boundary Set is a collection of computational objects whose organizational assignment cannot yet be confidently determined under the current Goal Function, available context, structural evidence, and computational architecture.**

The phrase

> **cannot yet**

is fundamental.

Boundary Sets represent temporary or evolving organizational uncertainty rather than permanent uncertainty.

---

# 3. Boundary Sets Are Organizational Objects

A Boundary Set is not simply another dataset.

It is an organizational construct.

It explicitly records that:

* organizational evidence is incomplete,
* computational responsibility is unresolved,
* dispatch is uncertain,
* architecture may be insufficient,
* or additional organization is beneficial.

Thus,

```text
Boundary Set
≠
Data Remaining After Classification
```

Instead,

```text
Boundary Set
=
Explicit Organizational State
```

---

# 4. Boundary Sets Are Not Noise

Noise may exist inside Boundary Sets.

However,

Boundary Sets frequently contain:

* future specialist domains,
* cross-disciplinary computation,
* transitional runtime behavior,
* rare but important cases,
* new organizational structures.

Therefore,

```text
Noise
⊂
Boundary Sets
```

but

```text
Boundary Sets
⊄
Noise
```

Confusing these two concepts causes valuable organizational information to be discarded.

---

# 5. Boundary Sets Are Not Classification Errors

Many machine-learning pipelines interpret uncertain samples as errors.

GTDO interprets them differently.

The organization itself may be incomplete.

Therefore,

```text
Boundary
≠
Misclassification
```

Instead,

Boundary indicates

```text
Current Organization
is insufficient.
```

---

# 6. Boundary Sets Are Computational Responsibility Boundaries

The deeper interpretation is

```text
Boundary Set
=
Unresolved Computational Responsibility
```

Questions represented inside a Boundary Set include

* Which responsibility should own this computation?
* Should several responsibilities cooperate?
* Does a new responsibility need to be created?
* Is additional context required?
* Should this remain under General AI?

Boundary Sets therefore expose responsibility rather than statistical uncertainty.

---

# 7. Sources of Boundary Sets

Boundary Sets may arise from many causes.

Examples include

* weak evidence,
* insufficient context,
* overlapping responsibilities,
* multiple valid goals,
* runtime uncertainty,
* architectural limitations,
* newly emerging domains,
* contradictory supervision,
* sparse data.

Different causes require different resolution strategies.

---

# 8. Boundary Taxonomy

GTDO recognizes several common Boundary types.

## Weak-Evidence Boundary

Too little evidence exists.

## Context Boundary

Required context is missing.

## Mixed-Domain Boundary

Several computational domains participate simultaneously.

## Responsibility Boundary

Several Computational Responsibilities compete.

## Transitional Boundary

The sample lies between runtime stages.

## Emerging Boundary

Repeated unresolved structures indicate a new capability.

## Constraint Boundary

Safety, policy, hardware, or legal restrictions prevent assignment.

## General-Coverage Boundary

Specialization is unnecessary.

Each category suggests different computational responses.

---

# 9. Boundary Sets and Context

Many Boundary Sets disappear after context enrichment.

Example

```text
Input
+
Additional Context
        │
        ▼
Boundary disappears
```

However,

additional context may also reveal

* multiple valid responsibilities,
* composite computation,
* hierarchical organization.

Thus,

context both resolves and discovers organizational complexity.

---

# 10. Boundary Sets and Goal Functions

Boundary quality depends strongly on the Goal Function.

A weak Goal Function produces artificial boundaries.

An appropriate Goal Function produces meaningful organizational separation.

Therefore,

Boundary analysis is also Goal Function evaluation.

---

# 11. Boundary Sets and Dispatch

Dispatch must recognize Boundary Sets explicitly.

Possible dispatch outcomes include

```text
Specialist

General AI

Boundary Unit

Human Review

Multi-Path Dispatch

Deferred Dispatch

No Dispatch
```

Boundary becomes a legitimate dispatch destination.

---

# 12. Boundary Sets and Two-Way CCC

Two-Way CCC naturally produces Boundary Sets.

Cases with weak structural dominance remain unresolved.

Rather than forcing assignment,

GTDO preserves them.

Boundary preservation protects later architectural improvement.

---

# 13. Recursive Two-Way CCC

Some Boundary Sets contain additional hidden structure.

Recursive organization may produce

```text
Boundary
        │
Recursive Two-Way CCC
        │
───────────────
New Group A

New Group B

Residual Boundary
```

Recursion should only occur when new organizational evidence exists.

Otherwise,

recursive organization merely repeats the same uncertainty.

---

# 14. No-Split

Sometimes the correct decision is

```text
No Split
```

Forcing unnecessary specialization damages

* General AI capability,
* Operator Economy,
* organizational simplicity.

No-Split is therefore an explicit organizational decision rather than a failed split.

---

# 15. General AI as Boundary Coverage

Some Boundary Sets are best handled by General AI.

General capability provides

* broad coverage,
* robustness,
* adaptability,
* integration.

General AI should therefore remain an explicit organizational component instead of being treated as obsolete after specialization.

---

# 16. Boundary Computational Units

Persistent Boundary Sets may justify dedicated Computational Units.

Possible Boundary Units include

* Context Generator,
* Responsibility Coordinator,
* Multi-Path Dispatcher,
* Human–AI Coordinator,
* Boundary Brain Unit.

These units organize uncertainty rather than ordinary computation.

---

# 17. Human–AI Boundary Resolution

Humans are naturally effective Boundary processors.

Human contribution includes

* supplying missing context,
* redefining goals,
* approving difficult cases,
* discovering new computational organization.

Human resolutions should feed future GTDO organization.

---

# 18. Boundary Promotion

Persistent Boundary structures may evolve into

* new specialist groups,
* new Dispatch branches,
* new Brain Units,
* new Computational Responsibilities.

Boundary Sets therefore become sources of innovation.

---

# 19. Boundary Persistence

Boundary persistence is itself an organizational metric.

Persistent Boundary growth may indicate

* outdated architecture,
* poor Goal Functions,
* missing Computational Units,
* changing environments.

Boundary statistics therefore become architectural feedback.

---

Examples include

Hybrid AI makes Boundary organization even more important.

Different Computational Units possess different strengths.

Boundary processing decides

* cooperation,
* responsibility,
* dispatch,
* fallback,
* optimization,
* future specialization.

Hybrid AI therefore depends heavily on Boundary engineering.

---

# Long-Term Significance

Traditional AI attempts to eliminate uncertainty as early as possible.

GTDO instead records organizational uncertainty explicitly.

This seemingly small difference fundamentally changes AI architecture.

Boundary Sets become

* sources of architectural evolution,
* indicators of missing computation organization,
* generators of future Brain Units,
* foundations for Hybrid AI,
* and organizational bridges between today's AI and tomorrow's AI.

Rather than asking

> "Which group should this sample belong to?"

GTDO asks

> "What organizational information is this Boundary Set trying to teach us?"

This shift transforms Boundary processing from a cleanup operation into a primary mechanism of AI Computation Organization.

---

# Key Takeaways

* Boundary Sets are first-class organizational objects.
* They represent unresolved Computational Responsibility rather than merely uncertain classification.
* Boundary Sets should not automatically become Noise or Discard Sets.
* Context enrichment, recursive organization, and revised Goal Functions are primary Boundary-resolution mechanisms.
* No-Split is often the correct engineering decision.
* General AI provides important Boundary coverage.
* Persistent Boundary Sets reveal architectural evolution opportunities.
* Boundary processing is fundamental to Hybrid AI and general AI Computation Organization.
* Boundary engineering should be treated as a control-engineering discipline rather than a post-processing step.
